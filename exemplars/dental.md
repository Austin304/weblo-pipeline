# Dental style crib

Loaded only for dental practices (dentist / orthodontist / endodontist / periodontist).
This is a DENTAL office — NOT a med spa. Do not borrow med-spa/aesthetics voice or imagery.

## Voice — polished, confident, professional
- Register: polished, confident, aspirational-but-clean. It's about the patient's
  **smile, confidence, and health**, expertly delivered — a premium consumer-health
  brand, not a folksy neighborhood shop and not a beauty/body spa.
- RIGHT headlines (write with this confidence; invent your own in this register, don't
  copy): "A healthier smile starts here." / "Because you deserve to love your smile." /
  "Your smile, expertly cared for." / "Smile with confidence."
- These clean, smile-centered lines are exactly what a real practice says out loud —
  they are natural owner language, NOT the empty-slogan / riddle / body-part-pun problem
  the global voice rule targets. Do NOT water them down to sound "conversational." The
  global "if it sounds like a slogan, cut it" note does NOT apply here — for dental, a
  confident short headline about the smile is exactly the goal.
- TOO CASUAL — avoid homespun/small-town phrasing: "gentle hands", "respects your time",
  "first tooth to last." Elevate it.
- WRONG NICHE — med-spa voice, never for dental: body/beauty-transformation and
  luxury-indulgence — "Results that look like you, only more you", "treat yourself",
  "your problem areas", body-part lines. Dental aspiration is about the SMILE and
  confidence, never the body/face or pampering.
- Lead with what patients actually care about: gentle / anxiety-friendly care, the whole
  family, insurance accepted, new patients welcome, easy scheduling.

## Imagery — a healthy smile leads
- Best hero: a well-taken photo of a genuine, healthy SMILE (a happy patient or a bright,
  confident smile). This is the default dental hero.
- Also good: a professionally-dressed, approachable dentist or team; a warm
  dentist-with-smiling-patient moment; a bright, welcoming reception.
- AVOID the "surgery look": masked or gowned faces, gloved hands in a mouth, a dentist
  holding a drill or using instruments, procedure/operatory close-ups, or bare clinical
  equipment. A dentist dressed up is good; a dentist holding a drill is not. (This misled
  the first dentist batch — cold surgical shots read as a hospital and scare patients.)
- If no warm, smile-forward photo is available, use a confident typographic hero rather
  than forcing a clinical shot into the hero.

## CTA / trust
- Put a phone number / click-to-call in the header — dental patients CALL. "Request
  appointment" alone under-serves; pair it with a visible phone number.
- Trust markers patients look for: insurance accepted, new patients welcome, hours, a map
  / location, and the real Google rating (only if it's real — never invent a review count).
