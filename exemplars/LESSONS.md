# Lessons from hand-graded samples

Recurring flaws and confirmed wins from human review. Apply every relevant one.

## Photos (the #1 score-killer)
- **Match imagery to the ACTUAL service.** A dental practice must NOT show a wooden sauna,
  a gym, a massage room, or a med-spa/injectables scene — that mismatch reads as a
  stranger's business and embarrasses the owner. Pick imagery that plausibly depicts THIS
  specific service type; if unsure, prefer a clean, neutral, on-tone interior or a close,
  elegant detail over a wrong-modality scene.
- **Never hero a parking lot, storefront exterior, street view, power lines, signage,
  or a logo shot.** Those actively cheapen a premium feel. If the only real photo is
  like that, go typographic/atmospheric for the hero and use the real photo small.
- **Never reuse the same photo twice on one page** (e.g. hero repeated in a later
  section) — it reads as filler.
- A single strong, well-cropped photo beats three mediocre ones.

## Hero
- No "app-store card" hero (stacked name + one-liner + star badge). Star ratings live in
  the testimonials/trust area, never as a hero badge.
- Any text over a photo needs a real scrim/gradient so every word is legible — thin or
  low-contrast overlay text is a fail (kickers over bright highlights disappear).
- The headline must scale with clamp() and never clip, collide with the CTA, or smoosh
  on a phone.
- **Do NOT lay the headline directly across a tight face close-up.** Text over someone's
  eyes/mouth reads awkward and is hard to read. Either use a full-bleed *scene/interior*
  hero for overlaid text, or keep the face photo in its own panel (split hero: text on a
  solid/dark side, portrait on the other) with a clear, uncluttered text zone. A calm,
  atmospheric interior hero (see the City Cave "Find your calm" build) beat a
  text-over-face hero.
- **A smiling person beats an empty room.** For dental especially, a genuine
  smiling-patient / warm team photo is a stronger hero than a plain, EMPTY office or
  reception interior. An empty-room hero is passable-but-never-amazing; when a good
  smile is available (they usually score well), lead with it and use the room shot small.

## Variety (they must not all look alike)
- Do not default every build to the same generic centered serif template.
  Commit to the build's assigned DESIGN LANGUAGE and let structure vary: asymmetric
  splits, offset image blocks, full-bleed accent bands, real whitespace — no two
  adjacent sections sharing a layout.
- But EDITORIAL ≠ RANDOM: asymmetry must be deliberate and grid-aligned with consistent
  margins. If a section looks randomly placed, off-centered, or haphazardly stacked, it
  reads broken, not premium. Align related elements to shared edges/baselines.

## Layout / sections
- **"What we do" / services should be COMPACT and side-by-side, not an endless scroll.**
  A tight two-column / paired card block the visitor can scan in one screen beats a long
  single-column list they have to scroll forever through. Keep the core services above the
  fold-ish; don't stretch a handful of services into a full page of vertical stacking.

## Typography
- **No cursive / script or heavy-italic display face for body-length text — especially
  testimonial quotes.** A whole review set in large italic Playfair (or any calligraphic
  face) reads as fussy and hurts legibility. Set quotes and other running text in a clean,
  readable face — the sans, or a restrained UPRIGHT serif. Reserve decorative italics for a
  single short accent word, never a full sentence or paragraph.

## Copy voice (graded hard)
- Natural, warm, calm, relaxed — like a real grounded person, not a brochure. Short
  confident sentences a native speaker would say; read aloud and cut anything stiff,
  translated, or ESL-ish.
- **Use the plain, everyday phrasing of a common idea — don't invent a quirky near-miss.**
  The generator writes stilted variants like "we'll find a time that fits your week" when a
  real person says "we'll find a time that fits your schedule." When a normal idiomatic way
  to say something exists, use it verbatim; never swap in an odd twist just to sound
  different — it lands as unnatural and cheapens the whole page.
- Be GOOD AT DESCRIPTION: concrete, lightly sensory lines about the real experience —
  not vague abstract benefit-speak. Ban hollow filler ("the kind of attention that feels
  personal", "a considered approach", "elevate your journey").
- Personal, NOT invasive: know when personal matters and never overdo it — nothing that
  feels like wanting to "crawl inside their skin" or fixates on the body ("around your
  face", "your problem areas"). Warm, at a respectful distance.
- Plain labels beat flowery ones ("Why us", "What we do", "Visit us"). Match: "Still
  every bit you." / "Results that look like you, only more you."
- **BAN the "Real ___, real ___" template and its fragments.** The generator overuses
  "Real people, real results" and then mutates it into standalone nonsense like "Real
  hands" or "Real legs" — which is unsettling (it implies the alternative is FAKE hands).
  Never pair "Real" with a body part or tool. Write a plain human section header instead.
- **No cutesy body-part metaphors as headlines.** "Lighter legs", "around your face",
  etc. read as things no real person would say and are faintly creepy. A headline must
  be a sentence the owner could say out loud to a client without cringing — plain,
  warm, and about the person's experience, not a pun on their anatomy.
- Every headline test: would a real, grounded owner actually SAY this to a customer? If
  it sounds like a slogan, a riddle, or a body-part pun, cut it and say the plain thing.
- **Don't hang a sub-description under every heading.** Not every card, feature, or
  section label needs an explanatory blurb beneath it. A grid where each short label has a
  sentence pinned under it reads as trying too hard and dilutes the voice. Let items stand
  on a strong label alone and write a supporting line only where it genuinely adds
  something — fewer, better lines beat a blurb in every slot.
- **Don't lean on one mood-word.** Don't repeat the same adjective across the hero, a
  section heading, and the closing line (the generator overuses "unhurried" exactly this
  way). Once a word has carried the page's tone once, say the next thing differently;
  reusing it three times makes the copy feel formulaic and strained.

## Honesty (never trade this for polish)
- State only the facts in LOCKED CONTENT. No invented amenities (parking, refreshments,
  financing), credentials, awards, or **prices** — reference sites show pricing/specials
  cards, but we only have them if the data does. Use those layouts WITHOUT numbers.
- No ranking superlatives ("best", "#1", "top-rated", "award-winning") unless a real
  review says it verbatim.
