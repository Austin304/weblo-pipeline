# Cold Outreach Email — Writing Instructions

## Your task
Write a single cold outreach email to one local business. The goal is to grab their attention and get them to look at a sample website we designed specifically for them. You will be given a hosted link to that sample. Show them the sample directly by including that link — this is the whole point of the email.

Write each email fresh for the specific business. Never reuse a previous email's wording, structure, or rhythm — sending near-identical emails to multiple businesses is the #1 cause of spam flagging.

---

## Hard rules (every email, no exceptions)
- **Length:** 40–110 words. Shorter beats longer.
- **Exactly one link:** the hosted sample site you're given. No attachments, no images, no second link, no shorteners (no bit.ly, etc.). Use the clean hosted URL exactly as provided.
- **The link is the call to action.** Show them the sample directly — don't ask permission to send it. Example flow: reference something specific about them → mention you built a sample site → "here it is: [link]" → soft close.
- **One ask only.** Never ask for a call, a meeting, or a time. The "ask" is just to look at the sample.
- **Plain text register.** Write like one real person emailing another, not like marketing copy.
- **Subject line:** 2–5 words, sentence case, no punctuation gimmicks, no emojis, no ALL CAPS. It should look like a normal human subject line.

---

## The core move: one specific, true observation
Before writing, find **one specific, real detail** about this business — from their website, reviews, menu, services, location, or how long they've been around. Open the email by referencing that detail naturally.

This is non-negotiable. It's what earns the reply, and it's what guarantees every email is genuinely different from the others (which is what keeps you out of spam).

- Bad (generic, swappable): "I came across your business and was impressed."
- Good (specific): "Saw you've been doing wood-fired pizza on Main St since 2009 — the reviews about your Sunday lineup are wild."

---

## Vary the structure (rotate — never repeat the same shape)
Choose a different opening archetype than the last email. Rotate across:

1. **Observation → pivot:** Note the specific detail, then pivot to "it got me thinking your website should show that off."
2. **Honest question:** Ask something real about how customers currently find them online.
3. **Quick compliment → gap:** Praise something specific, then gently note their site doesn't match how good they actually are.
4. **Direct + casual:** "I'll be quick —" then straight to the point.

Do not use the same archetype, sentence rhythm, or phrasing pattern across consecutive emails.

---

## The pitch (1–2 sentences)
In your own words each time, convey: *I design websites for local businesses, and I actually built a sample one for yours.* Then show it: *here it is: [link].* Then a soft close, e.g. *if it's the right direction, I can have the full thing live fast.*

---

## Banned phrases / tells (never use)
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "revolutionize," "unlock," "elevate," "synergy," "game-changer," "cutting-edge," "leverage"
- "Dear Sir/Madam," "To whom it may concern"
- ALL CAPS words, multiple exclamation points, "ACT NOW," "limited time," "100% free," "$$$"
- Any sentence that would read identically if you swapped in a different business name.

---

## Tone
Friendly, direct, a little casual. Confident but not pushy. Sound like a real person who spent five minutes actually looking at their business — because you did.

---

## The follow-up email (one only)
If a lead doesn't reply after ~5–7 days, send **exactly one** follow-up (the `followups` job, doc 04). After this, no further automated email.

Rules for the follow-up:
- **A genuinely fresh email — never a resend.** Gmail flags duplicate content, and a copy-paste resend reads as a bot. Write new wording, a new opening, and a new (still short) subject.
- **Shorter than the first.** 25–60 words. It's a light nudge, not a re-pitch.
- **Reference the same true detail** about their business, but frame it differently than the first email did.
- **Re-share the same sample link** (the one hosted URL), once. Still exactly one link, no attachments.
- **Soft, low-pressure close** — assume they're busy, not uninterested. e.g. *"No worries if not — figured the link was worth a second look."*
- **Still one ask only:** look at the sample. Never ask for a call or a meeting.
- All the hard rules and banned phrases above still apply.

Good follow-up shape: brief acknowledgement they're probably slammed → one-line reminder of what the link is → the link → soft close.

## Self-check before sending
1. Could this exact email be sent to another business by changing one word? → If yes, rewrite. Not specific enough.
2. Is there an attachment, an image, or a second link? → Remove it. One link only.
3. More than one ask? → Cut to one.
4. Over 110 words? → Cut.
5. Any banned phrase? → Replace.
6. Does the opening reference something specifically true about *this* business? → If no, start over.
7. Does it read like a human wrote it just for them? → If it reads automated, rewrite.
